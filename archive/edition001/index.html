Purepoint UraniumTSXV:PTU10 Sep

Purepoint Reports Winter 2026 Assays from Dorado JV's Nova Target

The week's only chemical assay, not just a gamma reading. Nine holes at the Nova discovery on the 50/50 Dorado JV (with IsoEnergy), eastern basin margin. Best result is NV26-05, 0.3 m at 1.65% U₃O₈ from 390.4-390.7 m, paired with a 61,070 cps average / 73,070 cps peak on the downhole Mount Sopris 2PGA-1000 probe. NV26-03A returned a broader 3.9 m at 0.04% U₃O₈ (412.6-416.5 m) with a 0.4 m higher-grade inclusion at 0.10% U₃O₈, plus a separate 0.4 m interval at 0.79% U₃O₈ (37,050 cps) from 418.2-418.6 m. Seven more holes (NV26-01, -02, -04, -06A, -07, -08, -09A) returned lower-grade mineralization, 0.01-0.07% U₃O₈. Collars are tabulated in NAD83 UTM Zone 13 (e.g. NV26-05 at 548809E, 6475821N). The release ties this to previous Nova drilling of up to 8.1% U₃O₈ over 0.4 m within a broader 1.62% U₃O₈ over 2.1 m, and describes a mineralized corridor extending roughly one kilometre. The summer program at Nova has also been completed, with those results still pending.

IsoEnergyTSX:ISO8 Sep

IsoEnergy Completes Summer Drilling at Larocque East, Intersecting Widespread and Strongest Radioactivity to Date Along the Hurricane South Trend

26 holes for 10,159 m, and every number in the release is a counts-per-second reading, not a grade. The program overran its 8,000 m / 20-hole plan and brings 2026 to 16,963 m in 43 holes. Best intervals are LE26-250 on the West L Fault, 3.5 m at 11,075 cps from 341.0 m including 0.5 m at 43,160 cps; LE26-273, 3.0 m at 14,135 cps from 328.0 m including 1.0 m at 36,292 cps; and LE26-254 on the East L Fault, 2.0 m at 10,110 cps from 331.5 m. Eighteen of the 26 holes returned at least 350 cps over half a metre, 13 exceeded 1,000 cps. These are handheld RS-125 spectrometer totals taken on core - not a chemical assay, and not calibrated downhole gamma either, so they cannot be read as a percentage or converted into one. What they do establish is spatial: mineralization is now widespread along the Hurricane South trend rather than confined to isolated holes. Samples are at SRC with no stated turnaround. What's missing: any assay from the summer program, any cps-to-grade correlation for these holes, and a date for either.

Announces $2.0 Million Brokered Private Placement Led by Centurion One Capital

A modest raise with no catalyst attached. Up to 14,285,714 units at $0.14 (Centurion One Capital as lead agent, 8% cash fee plus broker warrants), with the agent holding an option for a further $300,000. Each unit carries a warrant exercisable at $0.22 for three years, with an acceleration clause if the stock trades above $0.44 for 15 straight days. Proceeds are earmarked for capital expenditures and general working capital rather than a named program at South Falcon East, and the release ties the raise to no specific drill or permitting milestone. Expected to close around 5 October, subject to CSE approval and the standard four-month-and-a-day hold.

Terra North ResourcesTSXV:GEMC8 Sep

Prominent Radiometric Anomalies Identified at Charlot-Neely Lake

First look at a new airborne dataset over Terra North's Charlot-Neely Lake ground, about 20 km north of Uranium City. 2,441 line-km of high-resolution radiometric and magnetic survey across 163.7 km² resolved seven uranium-thorium radiometric anomalies, one of them north of Neely Lake where historical outcrop sampling reportedly ran up to 0.8% U₃O₈. No anomaly-by-anomaly dimensions or coordinates are given, and radiometrics read surface and near-surface signal only, not depth. The release comes from Global Energy Metals (TSXV:GEMC), which holds a 0.5% NSR royalty across Terra North's Saskatchewan portfolio rather than operating the ground - Terra North itself is private, holds an option on the project from Fulcrum Metals Canada Ltd., and is 39.5%-owned by Terra Balcanica Resources (CSE:TERA). New to this brief on both counts.

Green Canada UraniumTSXV:GCUC9 Sep

Mobilizes Drill to Marshall Project, Athabasca Basin, Saskatchewan

First drilling under Green Canada's own name, on its first day trading following an RTO. Two holes are planned at the 11,225-hectare Marshall property, roughly 30 km southwest of CanAlaska's West McArthur Pike Zone, testing an unconformity-style target beneath sandstone cover. The company holds Marshall through a 51% option into North Millennium's underlying interest. No metreage, timeline or target dimensions are given - this is a mobilization notice, not a results release.

Rigs already turning, started just before the window Xcite Uranium (CSE:XRI) and Eagle Plains (TSXV:EPL) began a 1,200 m, three-hole program at the past-producing Lorado project on 1 September, testing coincident EM conductor and magnetic-break targets flagged by radon and soil geochemistry. Lorado mined 95,000 t at 0.19% U₃O₈ between 1957 and 1960, Beaverlodge-type structurally controlled mineralization. No assays yet. Dropped out of the ranked list this edition only because the start date fell out of the seven-day window.
Majors: one drill start, and an unusually quiet week otherwise CanAlaska Uranium (TSXV:CVV) mobilized a single rig to Key Extension on 8 September, 100% owned, about 15 km south of the Key Lake mill. Six to ten holes are planned to late October, testing up-ice of the Orchid Lake boulder field along a western conductive trend with more than 10 km of sparsely tested conductive stratigraphy. Winter holes KEY017 and KEY018 hit graphitic stratigraphy with semi-brittle faulting and localized chlorite-clay alteration; those geochemical results are still outstanding. No metreage, budget or contractor given. Historical Orchid Lake boulder grabs ran up to 0.13% uranium, and the release itself flags them as selective and not representative.

Cameco, Orano, Denison, NexGen and Uranium Energy Corp issued no releases in the window. NexGen held its investor day on 1 September as a pre-recorded event on Rook I construction but published nothing alongside it. UEC has been silent since 23 July; the Roughrider pre-feasibility study is still unreleased, with the last substantive status the June quarterly that had drilling roughly 80% complete and Tetra Tech engaged. UEC's fiscal year ends 31 July, so full-year results around October are the next scheduled opening for it.
Community agreements and road access, the class of news this brief has not been carrying Three items from late August that no aggregator on the news sweep reported, all of them ahead of the permitting decisions they gate. Paladin Canada and Birch Narrows Dene Nation signed a Mutual Benefits Agreement covering Patterson Lake South for the life of the project (31 August). IsoEnergy signed an Exploration Agreement with Kineepik Métis Local Inc. on 24 August, a framework for engagement, information sharing and community participation in exploration. And on 25 August the Clearwater River Dene Nation activated an industry monitoring checkpoint on Highway 955 north of La Loche, citing what it called an unprecedented pace of uranium exploration in its traditional territory. Highway 955 is the only all-season road into the southwestern basin, so that last one is an access and logistics fact for every operator working west of Cluff Lake this season, not a single company's story. All three sit just outside the 2-8 September window and are carried here as context.
Out of basin, but on the watchlist ATHA Energy (TSXV:SASK), 8 September, Rib North at Angilak, Nunavut. Seven holes (RIBN-DD-010 to -016A) extend the mineralized corridor from the 300 m defined at discovery in 2025 to 1.45 km of strike on the eastern limb, open in all directions. RIBN-DD-011 returned 20.0 m of composite mineralization over fifteen zones between 494.0 m and 700.5 m, including a 1.5 m interval at 661.5-663.0 m averaging 8,453 cps with a 26,680 cps peak; RIBN-DD-013A returned 13.0 m composite over five zones, RIBN-DD-014 7.0 m over six. Every number is a downhole total-count reading from a Mount Sopris 40TGU-1000 probe, not a calibrated eU₃O₈ conversion and not a chemical assay, so none of it converts to a percentage - the company's own thresholds are 300 cps for mineralization and 10,000 cps for high grade. Angilak is Archean-hosted vein-style mineralization in the Kivalliq, geologically unrelated to the basin. Carried because ATHA holds Gemini and a large basin package; not ranked against basin results.

North Shore Uranium (TSXV:NSU), 3 September, Rio Puerco, New Mexico. Phase 1 returned 3.8 m at 0.331% eU₃O₈ and 8.2 m at 0.295% eU₃O₈, both gamma-derived and unverified by assay, from a Morrison Formation sandstone roll-front in the Grants district - nothing in common with the basin's unconformity and basement-hosted style. Carried because North Shore is a basin operator (Falcon, West Bear).

Manhattan Uranium Discovery (TSXV:MANU), 10 September, Lisbon Valley, Utah. A compilation of 499 historical drill holes returned a best of 3.70% eU₃O₃ over 5.2 ft, gamma-derived from legacy data, not new drilling. Sandstone-hosted Colorado Plateau style, unrelated to the basin. Carried because Manhattan operates Murmac and Strike on this roster.

Ground coming open: 49 dispositions inside seven days, 270 inside thirty Eagle Plains Resources is the largest single block reaching its good-standing date by 16 September, at 13 dispositions, followed by Orano Canada with 11 and Mine-Find Geochem with 6. Golden Band Resources has 4, Skyharbour and Argo Gold 2 each, and there are singles from Greenridge, GEM Oil and ATHA Energy. Widen the horizon to 30 days and the count is unchanged at 270, still dominated by a 127-disposition block registered to one individual, Ryan Kalt, with Greenridge Exploration on 18, Eagle Plains and Orano on 13 each, Murchison Minerals on 12 and CanAlaska on 7. A block that size held personally and expiring together is the single largest piece of ground scheduled to come open in the basin this autumn.

Saskatchewan does not warn a holder before a claim goes: the registry manual states a claim lapses without notice where work credits are short and no deficiency deposit has been made. So the good-standing date is a hard deadline rather than the start of a grace period. The 278 dispositions currently showing an expired date while still flagged active are almost all recent - 275 of them fell due this year, 195 within the last 90 days - which is the registry catching up rather than ground still held. The pink outlines on the map are the seven-day set.
